# Chasem DJ: Outreach Strategy (one page)

**Goal:** Paid gigs around Boston. Start with Greek life and student orgs at Bentley and Babson, then neighboring schools, then bars and venues.

## 1. Referrals from your brother's frat are the fastest path
- You already have proof: real parties of up to about 30 people at a Babson chapter. A social chair trusts "my friend's frat uses this DJ" far more than a cold DM.
- Babson's Greek community is small (roughly 6-9 chapters; three NPC sororities: Chi Omega, Kappa Kappa Gamma, Sigma Kappa). Its chapters co-host and mix with each other, so one good intro can reach the whole system.
- Two newer Babson chapters (Phi Delta Theta, installed 2022; Theta Chi, reinstalled 2025) are still building their social calendars and vendor lists.
- **Ask:** after every gig, ask the host for two names. Use template #3. Offer a small discount for referrals that book.
- Bentley (your own school, about 20% Greek) comes next. Use your student status there: "I go here" lowers the barrier.

## 2. How to approach social chairs (timing matters)
- **Who:** the social chair, or the events/formal chair for sororities. Find them through the chapter's IG bio, highlights or posted exec board, or ask a member.
- **When:** chapters plan formals and semi-formals weeks ahead. Fall date parties and semis typically run Oct-Dec; spring formals run Feb-Apr. Pitch 4-6 weeks before. **It's mid-October now, so pitch late-fall semis and date parties this week, and start spring-formal pitches in January.**
- **How:** one DM, one link, and a clear offer (rate and what's included). Follow up once after 5-7 days (template #5), then move on.
- Note: many chapters have school social-event policies (e.g., registered events, third-party vendor rules). Ask whether they need a contract or W-9 so you're easy to book.

## 3. Getting on campus programming boards' radar
- Each board books through a student committee plus a staff advisor, and payment usually runs through a contract or vendor form. Ask who handles vendor paperwork.
- **Bentley CAB:** about 50 events a year, through Student Programs & Engagement. Join or attend a CAB general meeting, and pitch late-night events or Spring Day side stages.
- **Babson CAB:** cab@babson.edu. It reportedly has the biggest student programming budget.
- **Brandeis CAB:** two miles away. Big Game party, Springfest, smaller dances.
- Later: Northeastern CUP, BU Programming Council, BC CAB, Tufts Programming Board/TUSC.
- Tactic: offer to DJ a low-stakes event (involvement fair, tailgate, late-night) cheap or free once, to become their "go-to student DJ."

## 4. What Boston bars typically need
- **21+ rules:** most bars are 21+. If you're under 21, you generally can't work as a DJ in a 21+ bar. Check with each venue; don't assume. 18+ events exist (e.g., the "College Clash" party at Big Night Live) but are mostly run by **promoters**, not the venue.
- **Promoters:** Boston college nights are often promoter-driven. The promoter hires the DJ and fills the room, so DM promoters, not only venue managers.
- **Bring a crowd:** venues book DJs who fill slow nights. Pitch with numbers ("I can bring 40+ from Bentley/Babson Greek life").
- **Gear and format:** confirm whether they have CDJs or a controller hookup; have a clean, unclipped 30-45 min mix ready.
- Start local: Moody St in Waltham (The Gaff, Joco's) for a student night, then Allston/Fenway/Faneuil Hall rooms.

## 5. Weekly routine (about 2-3 hrs, between classes)
| Day | Task (15-30 min) |
|---|---|
| Mon | Update targets.csv statuses; pick 5 new targets |
| Tue | Send 5 DMs/emails (templates #1, #2, #4) |
| Wed | Post a 30-60s clip to @chase_m.music; tag past hosts |
| Thu | Follow-ups on anything 5-7 days old (template #5) |
| Fri/Sat | Gig, or go to a campus event/bar night and meet people in person |
| Sun | Thank-yous and testimonial asks (template #6); ask for 2 referrals |

**Weekly targets:** 10 new contacts, 5 follow-ups, 1 new clip, 1 referral ask. Track everything in targets.csv.
