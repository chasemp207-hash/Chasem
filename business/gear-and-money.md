# Gear and Money

*Gear prices are approximate new US street prices (estimates, verify before buying). Used gear on Reverb, Facebook Marketplace, or Guitar Center Used often runs 30-40% less.*

## Gear for paid 30-150 person parties

| Item | Budget option (est.) | Mid-level option (est.) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DJ controller** | $250-$350 (e.g. Pioneer DDJ-FLX4, Numark Mixstream-type entry units) | $600-$1,300 (e.g. Pioneer DDJ-FLX6/REV-series, Rane Four-class) | You likely have one. Upgrade only once it limits you. |
| **Laptop + DJ software** | Use what you have; rekordbox/Serato free tiers | $10-$20/mo for subscriptions or streaming integration | Keep music **downloaded locally**. Venue Wi-Fi fails. |
| **PA speakers (pair, powered)** | $400-$700 pair (10"-12" powered tops) | $900-$1,600 pair (12" pro-brand tops) | Covers about 30-80 people indoors with no sub. |
| **Subwoofer (powered)** | $350-$600 (12"-15") | $800-$1,300 (15"-18") | Needed to feel like a "real" party at 75-150 people. Buy second. |
| **Speaker stands + bag** | $60-$120 pair | $150-$250 | |
| **Wired mic** | $30-$100 (SM58-type) | — | Formals need announcements. |
| **Wireless mic** | $80-$150 | $250-$400 | Add once you're doing formals. |
| **Cables** | $60-$120 total | — | XLR (2-4), RCA-to-XLR or TRS, IEC power, extension cords, power strip, phone/aux adapter. |
| **Lights** | $80-$200 (2 LED par/effect lights, small fog machine optional) | $300-$600 (bar of lights + stands + controller) | Check venue fog/haze rules (smoke alarms). |
| **Headphones** | $60-$100 | $150-$250 | |
| **Backup kit** | ~$50-$100 | — | Spare cables, USB stick with a ready set, phone with an offline playlist, spare power strip, gaffer tape, flashlight. |
| **Cases/transport** | $50-$150 | $200+ | Plus a cart or dolly. |

**Starter "full rig" totals (estimate):**
- **Budget:** about $1,300-$2,200 (controller you own + speakers + stands + mic + cables + lights + backup). Add $350-$600 for a sub.
- **Mid-level:** about $3,000-$5,000.

**Transportation check:** a pair of 12" tops + a sub + stands needs a car with real trunk space. Figure this out before booking "your PA" gigs.

## Rent first or buy?

**Rent at first** (or borrow the frat's system) for the first 3-5 "your PA" gigs:
- Basic PA rentals are roughly **$150-$350/day** by generic guides, and a small PA package is $300-$500/day at a non-Boston nonprofit ([VERA Project](https://theveraproject.org/audio-stage/equipment-rentals)). These are rough, unverified guides, so get a real quote from a Boston shop such as Boston Audio Rentals (Allston).
- Rule of thumb: **buy speakers once you've booked ~3 gigs that need them** or rental costs approach about 30% of their price. Until then, push for venue/client-provided sound and charge the lower tier.
- Buy used to save cash, and test it in the store.

## Track income and expenses

Use one Google Sheet with two tabs. Keep receipts (photo in a Drive folder).

**Gigs tab:** `Date | Client | Event type | Location | Hours | Fee | Deposit received (date) | Balance received (date) | Payment method | Add-ons | Miles driven | Repeat client? (Y/N) | Referral source | Notes`

**Expenses tab:** `Date | Vendor | Item | Category (gear / rental / music subscription / travel / parking / marketing / insurance) | Amount | Gig it was for (if any) | Receipt link`

Monthly: total income − total expenses = profit. Set aside **~25-30% of profit** in a separate savings account for possible taxes (rough rule of thumb, not tax advice).

Tip: use a separate Venmo business profile or a separate account so gig money isn't mixed with personal money.

## Things to check (not legal or tax advice)

### Taxes on self-employment income
- **Check:** Net self-employment earnings of **$400+** in a year generally means filing a federal return and owing self-employment tax (~15.3%), *even if you get no 1099* ([Stride Health](https://support.stridehealth.com/hc/en-us/articles/231452268), [Ecommerce Fastlane](https://ecommercefastlane.com/side-hustle-taxes-2026-us-thresholds/)). Verify on the IRS Gig Economy Tax Center.
- **Check:** Venmo/Zelle 1099-K federal threshold for 2026 is reported as **>$20,000 and >200 transactions** ([Beancount guide citing IR-2025-107](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/05/08/form-1099-k-2026-threshold-reverts-20000-200-transactions-obbba-payment-app-reporting-guide)). Income is taxable even below that. Check whether Massachusetts has its own lower threshold.
- **Check:** If your parents claim you as a dependent, how your gig income affects their return and your own filing.
- **Check:** Which gear and expenses are deductible business expenses.
- Good free resources: Bentley's accounting/tax faculty, VITA (Volunteer Income Tax Assistance) clinics, or a family accountant.

### Bentley rules for students running a business
- **Check:** Bentley's Student Handbook / Code of Conduct on operating a business, advertising on campus, or using campus spaces, Wi-Fi, or university branding.
- **Check:** Rules for performing at Bentley-sponsored or registered student events (approved-vendor lists, insurance requirements, W-9 forms).
- **Check:** Babson's and other schools' rules for outside vendors at Greek or org events. Some require vendor registration or a certificate of insurance.
- Ask Bentley's entrepreneurship center (look up its current name on bentley.edu) for guidance. They often help with exactly this.

### Liability insurance
- **Check:** whether venues or schools you play require a **certificate of insurance (COI)**. Many campus and bar venues do.
- Benchmarks: basic annual DJ general liability starts around **$192-$199/yr** at one seller, about **$59 per event** for one-off coverage, and gear coverage from about $7.50/mo ([Insurance Canopy](https://www.insurancecanopy.com/dj-insurance-cost)). An independent guide gives **$180-$740/yr** ([Fit Small Business](https://fitsmallbusiness.com/dj-insurance-buy-costs-coverage/)). Get multiple quotes.
- **Check:** whether your parents' homeowners/renters policy covers your gear away from home. Often it doesn't for business use.
- Suggested timing: per-event coverage when a venue requires it. Consider an annual policy once you're doing ~2+ gigs/month.

### Music licensing (basics)
- Reported general rule: for US public performances, the **venue/business** usually holds the ASCAP/BMI/SESAC licenses. Private, non-ticketed parties generally aren't treated as licensable public performances ([Entire Productions](https://entireproductions.com/blog/licensing-and-paying-for-music-performances-for-private-social-corporate-and-non-profit-events), [DJ Will Gill](https://djwillgill.com/?p=16580)). These are informal sources, so verify with the PROs.
- **Check:** for ticketed or paid-cover events, the host/venue may need licensing. Ask bars directly whether they're licensed.
- **Check:** that your music source allows **public performance**. Many consumer streaming terms don't. Use purchased tracks or DJ-licensed record pools/services.
