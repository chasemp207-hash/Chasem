## The brand rule: faceless at home

**No face in any bedroom video. On purpose.**
- At home the camera only sees: hands, controller, jog wheels, the laptop screen, the back of your head/shoulders, your silhouette, the gear and the room's lighting.
- Your face only shows up at parties. That's the payoff: people who want to see who's behind the decks have to come to a party.
- Faceless DJ pages are a real style. It keeps the focus on the mixing and adds some mystery.
- Your "signature" is your **hands + light color + gear**, so keep those consistent and recognizable: same watch/ring, same font, a light color you come back to.

**Face check on every take**
- [ ] Your face isn't in frame, even for a second (leaning in, adjusting the phone)
- [ ] No reflection of your face in the laptop screen, a mirror, the window or a framed poster
- [ ] Silhouette shots: your face reads as a dark shape, not visible features

## 1. Room setup (10 minutes)

### Clear the background (3 min)

- [ ] Nothing that reads as drinking or a messy dorm

- [ ] Keep 1 or 2 "character" items: a poster, records, a plant, the LED
- [ ] Tidy cables on the desk with tape or a twist tie

### Lighting (3 min)

**Rule:** turn OFF the overhead/ceiling light.
It makes flat, harsh light and looks like a dorm room.

**Night-time (best for the faceless vibe)**
- Warm lamp or LED BEHIND the desk/you, in the background
  → gives depth and a glow, and turns you into a silhouette
- Soft light in FRONT on the controller and your hands:
  desk lamp pointed at a wall (bounce), or a clip light
- For a silhouette: background light ON, front light OFF or very low
- Don't let any bulb point straight into the phone lens

**Daylight**
- Window in front of the controller (behind the phone) to light your hands and gear
- Silhouette in daylight: put the window BEHIND you and lock exposure on the window
- Use a lamp/LED in the background for color
- Daylight changes fast: film daylight shots first, night shots last

### Phone placement (2 min)
- Phone sits at the angle for the video (see each video below)
- Tripod or books must be solid: tap the desk, phone shouldn't shake
- Desk vibrates from speakers? Put the phone on a separate surface
  (shelf, chair, books on the floor)

### Framing for 9:16 + the safe zone (2 min)
- Always shoot **vertical** (phone upright)
- Use the **1x main lens** (best quality). Only use 0.5x if you can't fit the shot.
- Frame from about the **chest down**, or from **behind** your head. Never in front of your face.
- IG covers parts of the screen with buttons and captions:
  - **Bottom ~20 to 25%:** username, caption, audio. Keep hands/text out of here.
  - **Right edge ~15%:** like/comment/share buttons
  - **Top ~10%:** IG header
- Keep the jog wheels and your hands in the **middle area**
- The profile grid shows a cropped middle of the video, so center the action
- Turn on the grid lines in camera settings to help

### Phone camera settings (do once)
- [ ] Wipe the lens with your shirt/cloth (fingerprints = blurry video)
- [ ] Resolution: **4K at 30fps** (default pick: lets you zoom/crop in edit)
- [ ] Or **1080p at 60fps** for smoother hand movement on the close-ups
- [ ] Pick ONE frame rate for the whole day if you can
- [ ] iPhone: consider turning **HDR Video OFF** (Settings → Camera → Record Video). HDR can look washed out or too bright once uploaded.
- [ ] Lock exposure + focus before each take:
  - iPhone: press and hold on your hands → "AE/AF LOCK"
  - Android: tap and hold, or use Pro/Manual mode
  - Then drag exposure slightly down if lights/screen look blown out
  - Silhouettes: lock on the bright background, then drag exposure down
- [ ] **Do Not Disturb ON** and **Airplane mode ON** (no calls or buzzes mid-take)
- [ ] At least 20 to 30 GB free storage (4K takes up space)
- [ ] Phone plugged in or above 50% battery

## 2. Clean audio (keep it simple)

**Don't use the phone mic for the final sound.** It'll sound thin and distorted.
The phone mic is only a "guide track" for syncing.

### Main method: record in your DJ software, sync in the edit
1. In your DJ software, hit **Record** (rekordbox, Serato, djay etc. all have one) before each take. Or leave it recording the whole session.
2. Start the phone camera recording.
3. **Sync marker:** at the start of each take, tap a hot cue on a hard kick/clap.
   That sharp hit shows up in BOTH recordings.
4. Do the transition.
5. Export the recording as WAV/MP3 → AirDrop/send it to your phone.
6. In CapCut or Edits: put the video on the timeline, add the clean audio underneath, slide it until the sharp hit lines up with the phone's audio spike.
7. Mute the phone's original audio.

**Tips**
- Keep speakers at medium volume so the phone's guide audio doesn't distort
- Name each recording right away: "Mon intro take 2" etc.
- Check sync by watching your hand on the crossfader: the sound should change exactly when your hand moves

### Optional: cable straight into the phone
- If you already own an audio adapter/interface that connects your controller's master or booth out to your phone, you can try recording it directly.
- Test 10 seconds first. Some camera apps won't use external audio. If it doesn't work, go back to the main method. Don't buy anything for today.

### Fallback (if syncing is a headache)
- Line up the clean audio by eye: put the **drop** exactly where your hand moves the fader/hits the effect. Jog wheels don't need lip-sync precision.
- Still stuck? Use the phone mic, but: speakers at medium volume, phone close to one speaker, no talking, no fans/AC. Then raise the volume a bit in the edit.

### Do a 1-minute audio test before filming anything
- [ ] Record a 10-second test, sync it, play it back on your phone speaker AND earbuds
- [ ] Sounds clean? Go.
